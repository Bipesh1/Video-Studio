# What's the Catch? 03: Why Is the Water in Your Glass Older Than the Sun?

**TITLE:** Why Is the Water in Your Glass Older Than the Sun?

**THE QUESTION IN ONE SENTENCE:** How can the water in an ordinary glass be older than the Sun itself?

**THE WORLD:** One unbroken journey. A glass of water on a kitchen table, into one drop and one water molecule, back out through the window past the Earth and the Sun, then time runs backward until the Sun goes out and we are inside a dark, icy cloud. Time runs forward again as the cloud becomes the Sun and planets, and the ice rides a comet down to Earth and back into the glass.

- Kitchen table with a glass of water, a window behind it, a tap above
- A single drop on the glass rim, close up, frost able to creep over it
- Inside the drop: swirling water, then one water molecule (one oxygen ball, two hydrogen balls) that can open up
- A heavier hydrogen atom (slightly bigger, able to glow and to fade)
- Sky outside the window: sea, clouds, rain
- The whole Earth from space with its oceans
- An icy comet with a tail
- The Sun, blazing
- The Sun and planets shrinking into a glowing model on a computer screen, frost able to creep across it
- A dark, icy cloud in space, with specks of dust
- One speck of dust close up, a water molecule on it, a coat of ice forming
- The cloud falling in on itself into a spinning disk, the young Sun lighting up, planets forming
- A comet and rocks falling to the young Earth and its sea
- A shower spray and raindrops on a window (for the return to today)

## 3. FOLLOW THE CLUE

NARRATOR: So let's push into one drop, down to one water molecule, the tiniest bit of water.
[FLY: into the drop, through swirling water, down to a single water molecule | show: drop (on: drop), molecule (on: molecule)]

NARRATOR: Each water molecule is one oxygen atom holding on to two *hydrogen* atoms.
[OPEN: the molecule | inside: oxygen atom (on: oxygen), two hydrogen atoms (on: hydrogen)]

NARRATOR: But in about one water molecule in every three thousand, a hydrogen atom carries extra *weight*.
[CALLOUT: 1 IN 3,000 | caption: HAVE ONE HEAVIER HYDROGEN | at: one hydrogen atom, swelling slightly bigger | on: three]

NARRATOR: Scientists call that heavy water, and it is a *fingerprint* of where water was born.
[CALLOUT: HEAVY | caption: WATER'S FINGERPRINT | at: the heavier hydrogen atom, glowing | on: heavy]

NARRATOR: Our oceans carry that heavy fingerprint, and so do the icy *comets*.
[FLY: out of the drop, out through the window, up past the clouds to the whole Earth, then on to a passing comet | show: oceans (on: oceans), comet (on: comets)]

NARRATOR: But the Sun has much less, because heat wipes the fingerprint *away*.
[FLY: on past the comet toward the blazing Sun | show: Sun (on: Sun), the glowing heavier hydrogen fading in the heat (on: heat)]

## 4. THE CATCH

NARRATOR: So scientists rebuilt the newborn Sun and its planets inside a *computer*.
[FLY: the Sun and planets shrink into a glowing model on a computer screen | show: computer (on: computer)]

NARRATOR: But the newborn Sun and planets made nowhere near *enough* heavy water.
[CALLOUT: NOT ENOUGH | caption: HEAVY WATER MADE | at: the model on the screen | on: enough]

NARRATOR: Heavy water needs deep cold, close to the coldest cold there *is*.
[CALLOUT: DEEP COLD | caption: NEAR THE COLDEST POSSIBLE | at: the model, frost creeping across the screen | on: coldest]

NARRATOR: And the only place that cold was the dark cloud that came *before* the Sun.
[FLY: time runs backward, the Sun shrinks and goes out, and the camera drifts into a dark, icy cloud | show: cloud (on: cloud)]

NARRATOR: There, on a speck of dust, our water molecule froze into *ice*.
[FLY: deep into the cloud, close on one speck of dust | show: speck of dust (on: dust), our water molecule (on: molecule), a coat of ice (on: ice)]

NARRATOR: Then the cloud collapsed into the Sun and planets, and that ice *survived*.
[FLY: time runs forward, the cloud falls in on itself into a spinning disk, the Sun lights up | show: Sun (on: Sun), planets (on: planets), the iced speck still glinting (on: ice)]

NARRATOR: That ice rode to Earth on comets and rocks, and melted into the *sea*.
[FLY: follow a comet down to the young Earth | show: comet (on: comets), rocks (on: rocks), sea (on: sea)]

## 5. THE PROOF

NARRATOR: Astronomers at the University of Michigan published this in the journal *Science* in twenty fourteen.
[CALLOUT: 2014 | caption: PUBLISHED IN SCIENCE | at: the young Earth's sea | on: twenty]

[CALLOUT: 30-50% | caption: OF OCEAN WATER, AT MOST | at: the Earth's oceans | on: thirty]

**SOURCES:**
- Cleeves, Bergin, Alexander and colleagues, "The ancient heritage of water ice in the solar system", *Science*, 26 September 2014. A computer model of the young solar system could not make the heavy water found in oceans and comets, so some of that water must be ice inherited from the cloud before the Sun; estimates 30 to 50 percent of ocean water at the high end.
- University of Michigan news release, "The water in your bottle might be older than the sun", 25 September 2014. Says that water is roughly a million years older than the solar system, and that the hotter Sun erased the heavy water fingerprint while the source had to be very cold. https://news.umich.edu/the-water-in-your-bottle-might-be-older-than-the-sun
- Nature news, republished by Scientific American, "Earth Has Water Older Than the Sun", 2014. Plain account of the model and the 30 to 50 percent estimate. https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/earth-has-water-older-than-the-sun/
- Space.com, republished by The Christian Science Monitor, "Earth's water is pretty old", 2014. Notes 30 to 50 percent is the high-end estimate; the low end is at least 7 percent, which is why the script says "as much as". https://csmonitor.com/Science/2014/0926/Earth-s-water-is-pretty-old.-Does-that-make-alien-life-more-likely
- International Atomic Energy Agency standard for ocean water (VSMOW). Ocean water holds about 156 heavy hydrogen atoms per million, which works out to about one water molecule in every 3,200 carrying one.
