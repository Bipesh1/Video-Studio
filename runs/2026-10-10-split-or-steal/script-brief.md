# Video Script 02: Split or Steal

Biku's whole script as he gave it on 2026-10-10, with its on-screen notes, sound notes and sources. The spoken words alone are in `script-original.md`.

## Production details
- **Niche:** GAME THEORY
- **Kind:** Question video
- **One-sentence premise:** Two strangers must secretly choose to share a pile of money or steal it, and the viewer plays first, before seeing what real people did on a TV show and how one man escaped the trap.
- **Length:** 1:48 of speech in a 2:00 video
- **Spoken word count:** 280
- **Platform and size:** Both. One script; the studio makes both sizes, so keep the table and the two players in the middle of the frame.
- **Palettes:** warm-interior for the game table, danger for the moments a ball opens, cold-dawn for the ending
- **Depends on final running time:** nothing
- **Disclosure:** The voices are synthetic. Switch on the platform's label for altered or synthetic content.
- **Version note:** v2 cuts v1 from 3 minutes to 2 minutes. v1 is kept.

## Cast and voices
- **NARRATOR:** Unseen. Dry, warm and a little amused, like a friend leaning across the table to explain the trick.
- **YOU:** The viewer's figure. Silent. A plain stick figure in a green cap, sitting in the left chair in Beats 1 to 3.
- **THE STRANGER:** An invented character for the viewer's game, not a real person. A stick figure in a striped tie, slightly too friendly. Voice: smooth and quick.
- **SARAH, NICK and IBRAHIM:** Real contestants, shown by first name only, as on the show. Silent, because no exact words of theirs could be confirmed.

## How this one is told
The viewer plays the game first, against an invented stranger, and has to choose before any answer comes. Then the same table fills with real players from a real show. The carrier is **the pair of golden balls**, which sit on the table in every scene. The main place is the game-show table, with one short cut to a scoreboard.

## The script

### Beat 1. The table (0:00 to 0:12). Spine step: Hook
**NARRATOR:** Two strangers. One table. A pile of cash. Each holds two golden balls: split, or steal. They choose at the same moment, in secret. Take a seat. You are one of them.

**On screen:** A spotlit table with a tall stack of cash. Two golden balls in front of each chair, labelled SPLIT and STEAL. On "You are one of them", YOU drops into the left chair and looks straight at the camera.

**Sound:** A low game-show hum. A chair scrapes.

**Caption emphasis:** split, or steal, You are one of them

### Beat 2. The rules (0:12 to 0:24). Spine step: Why it matters
**NARRATOR:** If you both split, you share. If one splits and one steals, the thief takes it all. If you both steal, nobody gets a penny.

**THE STRANGER:** I'm splitting. I promise.

**NARRATOR:** Now choose.

**On screen:** Three quick pictures of the rules: the pile halved between two smiling figures; the whole pile sliding to one grinning figure while the other slumps; the pile vanishing while both stare. The Stranger puts a hand on his heart. Then CHOOSE NOW holds on screen for two silent seconds.

**Sound:** A ticking clock that stops for the two silent seconds.

**Caption emphasis:** the thief takes it all, I promise, Now choose

### Beat 3. The cold logic (0:24 to 0:38). Spine step: First half
**NARRATOR:** Here is the cold logic. If the stranger splits, stealing gets you double. If the stranger steals, you get nothing either way. So stealing is never worse. The stranger thinks the same. Two steals. Nobody wins.

**On screen:** A thought bubble above YOU shows both cases side by side. YOU's hand slides toward STEAL. The camera turns: the Stranger has the same bubble. Both open STEAL and the pile vanishes.

**Sound:** Two soft clicks, then an empty silence.

**Caption emphasis:** never worse, Nobody wins

### Beat 4. The real show (0:38 to 1:05). Spine step: Second half
**NARRATOR:** This game was real: a British TV show called Golden Balls. Economists studied two hundred and eighty-seven of its finals. About half the players split anyway. With only a few hundred pounds on the table, nearly three in four shared. And promises did something strange. Making one made a player far more likely to split. Hearing one changed nothing. The biggest prize ever, over a hundred thousand pounds? Sarah stole it.

**On screen:** The table fills with real players, round after round. A scoreboard counter settles at 53% SPLIT. A speech bubble saying "I promise" floats across the table and pops on the listener's unmoved face. Then the biggest pile of the video, tagged £100,150, slides to Sarah's side while the other player freezes.

**Sound:** Applause and groans alternating, a bubble pop, then a drumroll that stops dead.

**Caption emphasis:** About half, Hearing one changed nothing, Sarah stole it

### Beat 5. The man who changed the game (1:05 to 1:30). Spine step: The surprise
**NARRATOR:** Then a player named Nick did the opposite. He told Ibrahim he was going to steal, no matter what, and promised to share the money after the show. The host reminded Ibrahim that nothing forced Nick to pay. Think about it. If Nick steals, stealing wins Ibrahim nothing. His only chance is to split and hope. So he splits. The balls open. Nick split too.

**On screen:** Nick taps his STEAL ball and nods. Ibrahim throws his hands up and looks away. A thought bubble over Ibrahim shows his STEAL option greyed out. He pushes SPLIT forward. Both balls open in danger-red light: SPLIT and SPLIT. Ibrahim's jaw drops, then both laugh as the pile divides.

**Sound:** Tense strings, silence as the balls open, then laughter and applause.

**Caption emphasis:** going to steal, split and hope, Nick split too

### Beat 6. Change the table (1:30 to 1:48). Spine step: The ending
**NARRATOR:** Nick did not play the game better. He changed it. You play this game all the time: price wars, group projects, a shared kitchen. When you are stuck between trust and betrayal, do not ask which ball to pick. Ask how to change the table.

**On screen:** The table dissolves into three quick everyday versions: two shopkeepers cutting price tags at each other, students around one laptop, two flatmates and a sink of dishes, each with the two golden balls in the middle. Back at the original table, YOU pushes both balls aside, stands and walks round to the Stranger's side.

**Sound:** The hum from Beat 1 returns, softer. One clean note on the last word.

**Caption emphasis:** He changed it, change the table

## Why it passes the five tests
- **TRUE:** The rules, the study's figures, the biggest jackpot and the Nick episode all come from the sources below. Nobody real is given words that cannot be confirmed. The only speaking character besides the narrator is invented.
- **CLEAR:** "Would you share a fortune with a stranger, or steal it?" is understood in two seconds.
- **NEW:** The Nick and Ibrahim episode is widely discussed, so the episode alone is not new. The new angle is the order: the viewer plays first, then sees the economists' finding that promises persuaded only the person making them, then sees how Nick turned that finding upside down.
- **SURPRISING:** Five turns: the cold logic says both steal; real people split about half the time; promises do not persuade the listener; the biggest prize was stolen; Nick won by announcing he would steal.
- **IMMERSIVE:** The viewer sits at the table, hears the stranger's promise and has to choose before any answer comes.

## Notes on accuracy
- **"Stealing is never worse"** is exactly right for this show's rules. If the other player steals, you get nothing whether you split or steal.
- **"The cold logic"** stands for the textbook assumption that each player cares only about their own money.
- **The Nick episode's jackpot** is reported inconsistently, so the script gives no figure.
- **The episode's air date** could not be confirmed, so the script gives no date.
- **No exact words** from the real contestants could be confirmed, so the narrator tells their parts. "I'm splitting. I promise." is said by the invented stranger.
- **"Over a hundred thousand pounds"** is the £100,150 jackpot, rounded for speech.
- **Cut from v1 for length:** the show's dates and the finding that cooperation fell below half when the jackpot grew large.
