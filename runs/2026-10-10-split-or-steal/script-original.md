# Split or Steal

## Beat 1. The table (0:00 to 0:12)

NARRATOR: Two strangers. One table. A pile of cash. Each holds two golden balls: split, or steal. They choose at the same moment, in secret. Take a seat. You are one of them.

## Beat 2. The rules (0:12 to 0:24)

NARRATOR: If you both split, you share. If one splits and one steals, the thief takes it all. If you both steal, nobody gets a penny.

THE STRANGER: I'm splitting. I promise.

NARRATOR: Now choose.

## Beat 3. The cold logic (0:24 to 0:38)

NARRATOR: Here is the cold logic. If the stranger splits, stealing gets you double. If the stranger steals, you get nothing either way. So stealing is never worse. The stranger thinks the same. Two steals. Nobody wins.

## Beat 4. The real show (0:38 to 1:05)

NARRATOR: This game was real: a British TV show called Golden Balls. Economists studied two hundred and eighty-seven of its finals. About half the players split anyway. With only a few hundred pounds on the table, nearly three in four shared. And promises did something strange. Making one made a player far more likely to split. Hearing one changed nothing. The biggest prize ever, over a hundred thousand pounds? Sarah stole it.

## Beat 5. The man who changed the game (1:05 to 1:30)

NARRATOR: Then a player named Nick did the opposite. He told Ibrahim he was going to steal, no matter what, and promised to share the money after the show. The host reminded Ibrahim that nothing forced Nick to pay. Think about it. If Nick steals, stealing wins Ibrahim nothing. His only chance is to split and hope. So he splits. The balls open. Nick split too.

## Beat 6. Change the table (1:30 to 1:48)

NARRATOR: Nick did not play the game better. He changed it. You play this game all the time: price wars, group projects, a shared kitchen. When you are stuck between trust and betrayal, do not ask which ball to pick. Ask how to change the table.
