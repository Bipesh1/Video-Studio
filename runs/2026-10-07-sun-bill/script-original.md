# What If the Sun Sent You a Bill?

## Beat 1. The envelope (0:00 to 0:15)

An envelope slides under your door. No stamp. It is warm. Inside is a bill, and the name at the top is yours. Sender: the Sun. Amount due, for the last hour of sunlight on Earth: thirty trillion dollars.

## Beat 2. The sum (0:15 to 0:46)

You laugh. Then you check the sum. Take all the sunlight that lands on this planet in one hour. Price it like the electricity in your wall. Thirty trillion. About what the whole United States earns in a year. Every four hours, the Sun delivers the value of everything the entire world earns in a year. And then it does it again. In under ninety minutes, the Earth soaks up more energy than every city, car and factory will use this year.

## Beat 3. Your share (0:46 to 1:03)

You try the obvious defence. That is not all mine. Fine. Split it between every person alive. Your share is more than three thousand dollars. An hour. Thirty million dollars a year, delivered to you, for free, since the day you were born.

## Beat 4. The real bills (1:03 to 1:27)

Then you look at your real bills. Electricity. Fuel. Food. And you notice something. Coal is sunlight that plants caught hundreds of millions of years ago. Oil is the same story. Wind is air the Sun warmed. Rain is water the Sun lifted. Your dinner is this summer's light. You have almost never paid for energy. You have only paid for delivery.

## Beat 5. The savings account (1:27 to 1:42)

And the coal and the oil? That is a savings account. Sunlight, deposited a little at a time, over hundreds of millions of years. We found the account a few hundred years ago. We have been emptying it ever since.

## Beat 6. The second page (1:42 to 2:05)

So you turn the bill over, looking for the catch. There is a second page. It shows what this costs the sender. Every second, the Sun destroys four million tonnes of itself to make that light. And Earth catches almost none of it. One part in two billion. The rest pours into empty space, unbilled, unseen, for nobody.

## Beat 7. The account (2:05 to 2:18)

It has done this for four and a half billion years. Before your street. Before people. Before anything alive could look up. It lit oceans with nothing in them. No invoice. No reminder. No interest.

## Beat 8. The last line (2:18 to 2:45)

At the bottom there is one last line, and it is not a demand. Amount due: nothing. It was never a bill. It was a statement. Paid in full, by the sender. Remaining credit: about five billion years. You fold it up. Outside, it is morning. And for the first time in your life, you feel the Sun on your face and know exactly what it is worth.
