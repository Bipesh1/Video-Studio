
TITLE: Why a Fine for Being Late Made Parents Even Later
THE STRANGE ENDING IN ONE SENTENCE: A daycare started fining parents who picked their kids up late, more parents came late, and when the fine was dropped they kept coming late.
THE REAL RESULT: In a 1998 field study at daycares in Israel, a small late fine nearly doubled late pickups, and they stayed high after the fine was removed.
WHO IS IN IT: the Saint (the teacher), the Cheat (a father), the Copycat (a father), You (silent), a small boy (silent)
THE PLACE: a daycare front door at closing time; a street by day
NEEDS DRAWING: a daycare front door with a window and a notice board beside it; a small boy

1. THE STRANGE ENDING
(A daycare door at a quarter past four. A small boy waits alone at the window. The teacher stands at the door in her coat, keys in hand. A note on the board says NO MORE LATE FINE.)
TRAINER: Why did a fine for being late make parents even *later?* [BIG WORDS]
(A father in a red striped tie strolls up with a coffee, in no hurry.)
TRAINER: And when this daycare dropped the fine, they *still* came late.

2. REWIND
(Earlier. The same door, with nothing on the board. A father in an orange beanie runs up, out of breath.)
TRAINER: Earlier, there was no fine at *all.*
COPYCAT: I'm so *sorry!* It won't happen again.
SAINT: It's okay. My *last* bus is at four forty.
TRAINER: The teacher waited as a favor. Late parents paid only with a *sorry.*
(The teacher pins a new note to the board. It says LATE FINE $3.)
TRAINER: Then this daycare made one rule. Pick up your child by *four.*
TRAINER: Each time you're more than ten minutes late, you pay three *dollars.* [NUMBER: $3]

3. THE TRAP CLOSES
(The father in the red striped tie reads the note. A slow smile.)
CHEAT (thinks): So I can *buy* twenty extra minutes for three dollars. That's cheaper than a babysitter. [REACTION: greed]
(The next day. The clock says twenty past four. He strolls up and hands the teacher three dollars.)
CHEAT: Here's my *fee.*
SAINT: The fine is meant to make you *hurry.*
(He shrugs.)
CHEAT: I'm paying it, so we're *even.*
(The father in the orange beanie watches from the gate.)
COPYCAT (thinks): He didn't even say sorry. He just *paid.* So can I.
(The next week. The clock says half past four. The father in the orange beanie strolls up, in no hurry.)
COPYCAT: Put it on my *bill.*
TRAINER: Soon, more parents paid instead of saying *sorry.*
(The clock reaches four forty. The teacher checks her phone and sighs.)
TRAINER: The teacher missed her bus. A little boy waited *alone* at the window.

4. THE FREEZE
(A street by day. The cars are not moving. You is stuck among them and checks his phone. It says three fifty five.)
TRAINER: Now it's almost four, and *you* are stuck in traffic.
TRAINER: Being late now just costs three dollars. Do you still *rush?* [PAUSE]
(Silence.)

5. THE REVEAL
(Back at the daycare door. Parents stroll past the note with money in hand.)
TRAINER: The fine turned the teacher's *favor* into something you could buy. [WHAT YOU GET: NO FINE: SAY SORRY / $3 FINE: JUST PAY]
TRAINER: And nobody says sorry for *buying* something.
TRAINER: This is not a story. In 1998, two economists tried this at six *daycares* in Israel. [NUMBER: 1998]
TRAINER: Within a few weeks, late pickups there nearly *doubled.* [NUMBER: 2x]
(The teacher takes the note down. The parents keep strolling in late.)
TRAINER: When the fine was dropped, late pickups stayed just as *high.*
TRAINER: Now being late cost no money and no *sorry.*
TRAINER: The fine went to the owner. The teacher who waited got *nothing.*

6. THE STING
TRAINER: You do this too. When a late fee is small, you just *pay* it. [EVERYONE]
OUTSIDE VOICE: If you make a rule, skip the *small* fine. Ask as a favor, or make the fine big. [CARD: Making a rule? / Skip the small fine. / Ask as a favor, / or make it count.]
TRAINER: So why did the fine make parents *later?*
TRAINER: Because the fine didn't make them *sorry.*
TRAINER: It told them what sorry *costs.*

SOURCES:
- Uri Gneezy and Aldo Rustichini, "A Fine Is a Price", The Journal of Legal Studies, vol. 29, no. 1, 2000. In 1998, a fine for picking children up more than ten minutes late was added at six of ten daycares in Haifa, Israel, picked at random; late pickups rose for a few weeks and settled at almost twice the starting level, stayed there after the fine was removed, and did not change at the four daycares with no fine. The real fine was 10 shekels per child (under three dollars at the time; the story rounds it to three), and it was paid to the owner, not to the teacher who stayed late. https://rady.ucsd.edu/_files/faculty-research/uri-gneezy/fine.pdf
