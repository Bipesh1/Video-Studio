## Sources

- David Gale and Lloyd S. Shapley, "College Admissions and the Stability of Marriage", The American Mathematical Monthly, vol. 69, 1962. Showed that when one side does the asking, everyone ends up in pairs nobody wants to break, and every member of the asking side gets the best partner they could have in any such pairing. That the waiting side gets its worst such partner was shown soon after (D. G. McVitie and L. B. Wilson, "The Stable Marriage Problem", Communications of the ACM, 1971). As given in Biku's script.
- The Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel 2012, awarded to Alvin E. Roth and Lloyd S. Shapley for the theory of stable allocations and the practice of market design. https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2012/summary/
- Alvin E. Roth and Elliott Peranson, "The Effects of the Change in the NRMP Matching Algorithm", JAMA, 1997. The US residency match switched to an applicant-proposing version, where the doctors do the asking, starting with the 1998 match.
