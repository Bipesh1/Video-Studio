TITLE: Could Humans Evolve to Hold Their Breath Like Seals?
THE QUESTION IN ONE SENTENCE: Could the human body evolve to stay underwater far longer, the way a seal does?
THE WORLD: From you lying under the bathwater, down into your chest to the spleen, then out across a warm sea to a stilt village in Southeast Asia, under the waves to a diver on the reef, and back to the bath.
NEEDS DRAWING: a bathtub with a faceless figure under the water and a counter beside it; a cut-away chest with lungs, heart and spleen; the spleen as a sponge packed with red blood cells; the arms and legs with blood pulling back toward the chest; a seal underwater with its huge spleen glowing; a Bajau stilt village with houseboats; a faceless diver with a wooden mask on a reef, with fish; an ultrasound screen showing two spleens side by side; a tennis ball for scale; a DNA strand with one glowing piece; a floating journal page; a pool with an oxygen tank beside it.

1. THE QUESTION
NARRATOR: Could your body ever evolve to hold its breath like a *seal*?
[FLY: close on a faceless figure lying under bathwater, a counter ticking beside the tub | title: COULD YOU HOLD IT / LIKE A SEAL? | show: seal shape in the ripples (on: seal)]
NARRATOR: The surprise is that, somewhere on Earth, it has *already* happened.
[FLY: down through the bathwater and out into open blue, a small turning Earth ahead | show: Earth (on: Earth)]

2. THE MOMENT
NARRATOR: Most of us come up for air within about a minute, and think that is our *limit*.
[CALLOUT: 1 MIN | caption: WHEN MOST OF US COME UP | at: the counter by the tub | on: minute]
NARRATOR: But your body hides an old diving trick, and it starts in your *chest*.
[FLY: into the figure's chest, past the ribs | show: lungs (on: chest)]

3. FOLLOW THE CLUE
NARRATOR: While you hold your breath, the oxygen in your blood slowly *runs down*.
[OPEN: the lungs | inside: blood vessels with oxygen bubbles fading (on: oxygen)]
NARRATOR: So your body switches on its dive reflex: your heart slows, and blood leaves your hands and *feet*.
[FLY: from the heart out along the arms and legs | show: slowing heart (on: heart), blood pulling back from the hands (on: hands), and from the feet (on: feet)]
NARRATOR: Then your spleen, a small organ under your ribs, squeezes like a *sponge*.
[OPEN: the spleen | inside: red blood cells packed like water in a sponge (on: sponge)]
NARRATOR: It pushes spare red blood cells, which carry oxygen, into your blood like a *backup tank*.
[FLY: follow the red blood cells out of the spleen into the blood | show: a tank outline forming around them (on: tank)]
NARRATOR: Seals have enormous spleens for exactly this *reason*.
[FLY: out of the body, through the water, to a seal gliding underwater | show: its huge spleen glowing (on: spleens)]

4. THE CATCH
NARRATOR: And here is the catch: the Bajau, sea nomads of Southeast Asia, have already grown a *bigger* tank.
[FLY: up out of the water, across a warm sea, to a stilt village with houseboats | title: THE BAJAU / SEA NOMADS | show: houseboats (on: Bajau)]
NARRATOR: Their divers spend up to sixty percent of a working day diving for *fish*.
[FLY: under the surface to a faceless diver with a wooden mask on the reef | show: diver (on: divers), fish (on: fish)]
NARRATOR: Scientists scanned the spleens of the Bajau and of their farming neighbours, the *Saluan*.
[FLY: up onto the shore to an ultrasound screen | show: Bajau spleen (on: Bajau), Saluan spleen (on: Saluan)]
NARRATOR: The Bajau spleen was about fifty percent *bigger*, roughly the size of a tennis ball.
[CALLOUT: +50% | caption: BIGGER THAN THE NEIGHBOURS | at: the Bajau spleen on the screen | on: fifty]
NARRATOR: It was big even in Bajau who do not dive, so it is in their *genes*.
[OPEN: the Bajau spleen | inside: a DNA strand with one piece glowing (on: genes)]
NARRATOR: Scientists think it took only around a thousand years at sea, a *blink* for evolution.
[CALLOUT: 1,000 | caption: YEARS, SCIENTISTS THINK | at: the houseboats | on: thousand]

5. THE PROOF
NARRATOR: Melissa Ilardo's team published this in the journal *Cell* in 2018.
[FLY: up above the village to a journal page floating over the sea | title: CELL / 2018 | show: journal page (on: journal)]
NARRATOR: They also found a gene, PDE10A, that may make the spleen *bigger*.
[OPEN: the journal page | inside: the glowing DNA piece (on: gene)]
NARRATOR: Scientists think it works through a body hormone, but nobody has *proved* that yet.
[CALLOUT: ? | caption: NOBODY KNOWS YET | at: the glowing DNA piece | on: nobody]
NARRATOR: The breath hold world record is twenty nine minutes, but only after breathing pure *oxygen*.
[FLY: across the water to a pool with an oxygen tank beside it | show: oxygen tank (on: oxygen) | title: 29 MINUTES / WITH HELP]

6. NOW YOU'LL NOTICE
NARRATOR: Next time you slip under the water, your heart is already *slowing* down.
[FLY: back across the sea, into the bathroom, close on the figure under the bathwater | show: a slowing glow in the chest (on: heart)]
NARRATOR: Your body is still running a diving program far *older* than you.
[OPEN: the chest | inside: the spleen squeezing (on: diving)]
NARRATOR: So could humans evolve to hold their breath like seals? Slowly, yes, and one people is already *on the way*.
[FLY: up out of the bath as the counter stops | title: IT'S ALREADY / HAPPENING]
