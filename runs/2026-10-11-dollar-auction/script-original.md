# The Dilemma Table 02: The Dollar Auction

## The opening (added 2026-10-11 at Biku's asking; he approved these two lines)

NARRATOR: Would you bid a nickel for a dollar bill?

NARRATOR: Two bidders said yes. Watch what it cost them.

## Beat 1. Sold (0:00 to 0:10)

NARRATOR: Sold. One dollar bill, to the Cheat, for a dollar eighty-five.

NARRATOR: And the Copycat pays a dollar eighty. For nothing.

NARRATOR: So who won?

THE CHEAT: Me. Obviously.

## Beat 2. The tiny rule (0:10 to 0:27)

NARRATOR: Rewind. One dollar. Bids start at a nickel. Highest bid takes it.

NARRATOR: One tiny rule. Second place pays too, and gets nothing.

NARRATOR: A dollar for a nickel. Would you raise your hand?

THE CHEAT: Five cents.

THE COPYCAT: Ten cents.

## Beat 3. Past a dollar (0:27 to 0:48)

NARRATOR: Ninety-five cents from the Cheat. One dollar from the Copycat.

THE CHEAT: If I stop, I lose ninety-five cents. If I bid a dollar five and win, I only lose a nickel.

THE COPYCAT: If I stop, I lose a dollar. A dollar ten.

NARRATOR: Nobody is chasing the dollar now. They just can't afford to come second.

## Beat 4. The party game (0:48 to 1:09)

NARRATOR: The economist who published this game, in nineteen seventy-one, said to play it at a party, when spirits are high.

NARRATOR: He wrote that three to five dollars for one dollar was not unusual.

NARRATOR: In one business school class, a twenty-dollar bill went for two thousand dollars. A hundred times its worth.

## Beat 5. Where the money goes (1:09 to 1:30)

THE SAINT: Sorry. Where does all that money go?

NARRATOR: To me. I'm the one selling the dollar.

NARRATOR: A real website ran a close cousin of this game. One study counted twenty-six million dollars in profit.

NARRATOR: It even auctioned cash, and took in about two dollars for every dollar it gave away.

## Beat 6. Sixty cents (1:30 to 1:44)

NARRATOR: I'd rather you missed this part. A game theorist solved my auction.

NARRATOR: Give both players two dollars and fifty cents. Perfect logic says one opening bid of sixty cents. The other player stays silent.

## Beat 7. You played this today (1:44 to 2:03)

NARRATOR: The trap was never the dollar, it was the second bid.

NARRATOR: You played this today. That group chat argument nobody would drop. Every reply cost you more of your evening, and neither side could afford to come second.

NARRATOR: So. Who'll start me at a nickel?
