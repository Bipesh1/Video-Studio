# How to Make Someone Sit in Smoke

THE TRAINER: If a room filled with smoke, would you get up and leave?

THE TRAINER: You think you would. Watch this.

THE TRAINER: This man came here to earn twenty dollars. All he has to do is sit and wait.

THE COPYCAT: Twenty dollars just to sit here? Easy.

THE TRAINER: What he does not know is that everyone else in this room works for me.

THE TRAINER: I pay them to do one thing. Stay calm, whatever happens.

THE TRAINER: Now I let smoke into the room.

THE COPYCAT: Is that smoke?

THE CHEAT: It's nothing. Probably the kitchen.

THE COPYCAT: Nobody else looks worried. So it must be fine.

THE TRAINER: See what he did? He did not look at the smoke. He looked at the people.

THE COPYCAT: It's getting hard to breathe. Shouldn't we tell someone?

THE CHEAT: Relax. If it was dangerous, somebody would say so.

THE COPYCAT: Yeah. You're right. It's fine.

THE TRAINER: Now he has said it is fine. Leaving would mean admitting he was wrong.

THE COPYCAT: My eyes are burning. Is nobody going to move?

THE TRAINER: What do you think he does?

THE COPYCAT: They are all calm. I must be the one who is wrong.

THE TRAINER: He stays.

THE TRAINER: This is not a story. In 1968, two scientists did this for real.

THE TRAINER: Alone in the smoke, three people out of four got up and reported it.

THE TRAINER: With two calm strangers beside them, only one in ten did.

THE TRAINER: The smoke was the same. Only the faces changed.

THE TRAINER: You do this too. A fire alarm rings, everyone looks around, and nobody moves first.
