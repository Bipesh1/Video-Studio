4. THE TRICK

NARRATOR: Here is the trick: the buttons quietly tell you what a *normal* tip is.
[SCENE: coffee shop counter | title: THE BUTTONS DECIDE / WHAT IS NORMAL | show: tip buttons 20% 25% 30% (from: tip buttons) (on: buttons)]

NARRATOR: The shop picks the buttons, the screen shows them, and *your* choices start right there.
[FLOW: title: WHO PICKS / THE BUTTONS | THE SHOP (on: shop) -> PICKS 20% 25% 30% (on: buttons) -> THE SCREEN (on: screen) -> SHOWS THEM TO (on: shows) -> YOU (on: your)]

NARRATOR: When the buttons start at twenty, twenty starts to feel like the *cheap* choice.
[QUEUE: title: 20% NOW FEELS / LIKE THE CHEAP ONE | 4 people | sign: 20% 25% 30% | on: cheap]

NARRATOR: Then comes a second push: someone is standing right there, *watching* you choose.
[SCENE: coffee shop counter | show: faceless barista (on: someone), dotted line from barista to the screen (on: watching)]

NARRATOR: In a coffee shop experiment, people tipped *more* when the worker was in view.

NARRATOR: A study of thirteen million New York taxi rides found higher buttons raised tips about ten *percent*.

NARRATOR: So decide your tip before you reach the counter, and press *custom* if the buttons are not yours.

SOURCES:
- Pew Research Center, "Tipping Culture in America: Public Sees a Changed Landscape," November 2023. Survey of 11,945 US adults in August 2023: 72% say tipping is expected in more places than five years ago; 57% say they tip 15% or less at a sit-down restaurant; more oppose (40%) than favor (24%) suggested tip amounts on screens. https://www.pewresearch.org/2023/11/09/tipping-culture-in-america-public-sees-a-changed-landscape/
- Kareem Haggag and Giovanni Paci, "Default Tips," American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, 2014. Over 13 million New York City taxi rides: screens suggesting 20/25/30% instead of 15/20/25% raised the average tip by about 10%, but rides with the higher suggestions were over 50% more likely to get no card tip at all.
- Goh and colleagues, "Pro tip: Screen-based payment methods increase negative feelings in consumers but do not increase tip sizes," University of Nebraska-Lincoln preprint, 2022. In a simulated coffee shop, people tipped more when the server was visible than when absent.
- The New York Times, "How to Deal With the Many Requests for Tips," 2023. Tip prompts with suggested amounts now appear at casual counters, food trucks and even self-service kiosks.
- Bankrate, Annual Tipping Survey, 2025. Notes that 15% was traditionally the average tip, with 20% for excellent service.
- The coffee figures (90¢, $1.20, $1.80, "a third more") are plain arithmetic on a $6 coffee, not quoted from a source.
