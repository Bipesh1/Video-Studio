# Where Does Your Fat Go When You Lose Weight?

## The package

**TITLE:** Where Does Your Fat Go When You Lose Weight?

**THE QUESTION IN A STRANGER'S HEAD:** Wait, where does it go? It can't just disappear.

**THE PROMISE:** You will know the real exit door fat uses to leave your body.

**THE ANSWER IN ONE SENTENCE:** Most of the fat you lose leaves through your mouth, breathed out as an invisible gas, and the rest leaves as water.

**THUMBNAIL:** A person breathing out a big, bright, visible cloud, close up, the cloud filling half the frame. Words: **YOU BREATHE IT OUT**

**THE FIRST TEN SECONDS:** Bare feet on a bathroom scale, the number drops by one kilo and a little yellow blob of fat lifts off and vanishes. "When your bathroom scale drops by one kilo of fat, where does that fat actually go?" Then the camera pulls back to a crowd of experts, most of them marked wrong.

**WHY A STRANGER CLICKS:** Everyone has tried to lose weight or knows someone who has, and almost nobody can answer where the fat physically goes.

**KIND:** big-question

**THE WORLD:** One body and one bathroom. The camera starts on the scale, dives into the belly, opens a fat cell, opens its drop of oil, follows the leftovers through the blood to the lungs, flies out of the mouth into the room, and comes back down to the scale.

NARRATOR: To burn that oil, your body needs extra oxygen, which you breathe *in*.
[FLY: up through the chest to the nose | show: a stream of red oxygen beads flowing in (on: breathe), (the figure takes one big breath)]

NARRATOR: One kilo of fat needs nearly three kilos of oxygen, so your lungs do the heavy *lifting*.

NARRATOR: Your body pulls the oil apart and joins each piece to that *oxygen*.

## 5. THE PROOF

NARRATOR: Two scientists in Australia worked this out by tracking every *atom* in ten kilos of fat.
[FLY: from the treadmill to a giant old balance scale | show: two faceless scientists with clipboards (on: scientists), a ten kilo block of yellow fat on the left pan (on: ten)]

NARRATOR: Ten kilos of fat plus twenty nine kilos of oxygen went *in*.
[CALLOUT: 29 KG | caption: OXYGEN IN | at: the left pan | on: twenty | show: a red oxygen block landing beside the fat (on: oxygen)]

NARRATOR: Twenty eight kilos of carbon dioxide and eleven kilos of water came *out*.
[CALLOUT: 28 + 11 KG | caption: GAS AND WATER OUT | at: the right pan | on: twenty | show: a grey gas block (on: carbon), a blue water block (on: eleven)]

NARRATOR: Both sides weigh thirty nine kilos, so not a single atom went *missing*.
[CALLOUT: 39 = 39 | caption: NOTHING VANISHED | at: the beam | on: thirty | show: (the beam settles perfectly level) (on: missing)]

NARRATOR: And no, panting on the sofa will not burn more fat. You will just get *dizzy*.
[FLY: to the figure on a sofa, puffing fast (on: sofa) | show: little stars circling his head (on: dizzy)]

NARRATOR: So where did that kilo on your scale go? You breathed it *out*.
[FLY: down to the bathroom scale as the cloud drifts over it, ending on the same shot as the first line | show: scale (on: scale) | title: YOU BREATHED / IT OUT]

## SOURCES

- Ruben Meerman and Andrew Brown, "When somebody loses weight, where does the fat go?", BMJ, 2014 (349: g7257, doi 10.1136/bmj.g7257). Traced every atom in lost fat: 10 kg of fat needs 29 kg of oxygen and becomes 28 kg of carbon dioxide and 11 kg of water; 84% leaves through the lungs, 16% as water. Also reports their survey of 150 doctors, dietitians and personal trainers and the common wrong answers (energy, heat, muscle, faeces).
- UNSW press release, "When you lose weight, where does the fat go?", via ScienceDaily, 2014 (https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/12/141216212047.htm). The authors on the energy and heat misconception.
- AsianScientist, "Where Did The Fat Go?", 2014. Reports the authors' point that breathing more on its own does not make you lose weight.
- NPR, "When You Burn Off That Fat, Where Does It Go?", 2014. The 84% to 16% split, and the water leaving through urine, sweat and other body fluids.
