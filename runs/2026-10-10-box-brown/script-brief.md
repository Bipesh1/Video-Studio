# Video Script 01: The Man Who Mailed Himself Twice

Biku's whole script as he gave it on 2026-10-10, with its on-screen notes, sound notes and sources. The spoken words alone are in `script-original.md`.

## Production details
- **Niche:** HISTORY
- **Kind:** Story video
- **One-sentence premise:** In 1849 Henry Brown escaped slavery by having himself shipped north in a wooden box, and years later, in England, he climbed into a box again on purpose, in front of a crowd.
- **Length:** 2:50 of speech in a 3:00 video
- **Spoken word count:** 440
- **Platform and size:** Facebook, vertical, 1080 by 1920. Chosen because this is a Black history story for the Facebook pages.
- **Palettes:** danger, storm-slate, warm-interior, dusk-violet, cold-dawn
- **Depends on final running time:** nothing
- **Disclosure:** The voices are synthetic. Switch on the platform's label for altered or synthetic content.

## Cast and voices
- **NARRATOR:** Unseen. A calm, low, close voice that never hurries, warm without being sentimental.
- **HENRY BROWN:** Speaks only words recorded in the sources. Drawn as a Black man with a deep brown skin tone. From Beat 3 onward his left hand is wrapped in a white bandage, which makes him recognisable in every shot. Voice: steady, tired and polite.
- **THE ELDEST CHILD:** Speaks one documented line. A small Black child, standing up in a wagon.
- **A VOICE OUTSIDE:** One of the men waiting in Philadelphia. Speaks one documented word. Seen only as a hand knocking on the lid.
- **NANCY:** Silent. Henry's wife, a Black woman in a headscarf. Shown with dignity, standing upright, never cowering.
- **JAMES SMITH and SAMUEL SMITH:** Silent. James is a free Black man in a long coat. Samuel is a white shoemaker in a leather apron.

## How this one is told
The cast is small because the story is one man's: the narrator tells it, and real people speak only the words the sources record. The carrier is **the box**, which is on screen at the start, the middle and the end. The main place is the box itself, with short visits to a Richmond street, a workshop, the express route, a Philadelphia office and a hall in Leeds. The video opens in the middle of the journey, flashes back to why he went, and plants one line in the hook ("he will climb into a box like this again, on purpose") that pays off near the end.

## The script

### Beat 1. Upside down (0:00 to 0:22). Spine step: Hook
**NARRATOR:** A man is folded inside a wooden crate, and someone has set it down upside down. Blood pounds in his head. The veins at his temples swell as thick as a finger. One sound, and he is caught. Years from now, he will climb into a box like this again. On purpose. First, he has to survive this one.

**On screen:** Darkness, then a cutaway of a crate standing on its lid, with Henry curled inside, head down. Painted on the crate, the right way up for him but upside down for us: THIS SIDE UP. A thin line of light comes through three small holes by his face.

**Sound:** A loud heartbeat. Muffled wheels and engine noise outside.

**Caption emphasis:** upside down, One sound, On purpose

### Beat 2. The wagon (0:22 to 0:50). Spine step: Why it matters
**NARRATOR:** Richmond, Virginia, eighteen forty-eight. Henry Brown is enslaved. He has paid his wife's owner to keep his family together. The owner takes the money and sells them anyway: Nancy, pregnant, and their three children, south to North Carolina. A line of chained people passes in the street. From a wagon, his eldest child sees him.

**THE ELDEST CHILD:** Father! Father!

**NARRATOR:** Henry walks beside Nancy as far as he is allowed. Then the road takes her.

**On screen:** A street in storm-slate. Coins pass from Henry's hand to a white man in a top hat, who pockets them and points south. A long line of figures in chains walks past, heads up. A small child stands in a wagon and reaches out. Henry walks beside Nancy until a rope barrier stops him, and the line shrinks into the distance.

**Sound:** Rain, chains, wagon wheels. The child's cry. Then only rain.

**Caption emphasis:** sells them anyway, Father! Father!

### Beat 3. The plan (0:50 to 1:20). Spine step: First half
**NARRATOR:** Now he has nothing left to lose. A free Black friend, James Smith, helps him plan. A white shoemaker, Samuel Smith, agrees to ship him north for eighty-six dollars, more than half of all Henry has saved. To get a day off work, he burns his own hand with acid, down to the bone. The box is about three feet across. Inside go a bladder of water and three small air holes. On the lid: This side up.

**On screen:** A workshop at night in warm-interior. The three men lean over a crate. A tag on Henry's coin purse reads $86 OF $166. A brief, gentle cut: a small bottle, then Henry's left hand being wrapped in a bandage. No wound is shown. The lid is painted THIS SIDE UP.

**Sound:** A hammer tapping nails. A single sharp breath from Henry.

**Caption emphasis:** eighty-six dollars, down to the bone, This side up

### Beat 4. The journey (1:20 to 1:39). Spine step: Second half
**NARRATOR:** March, eighteen forty-nine. A wagon, a train, a steamboat, a ferry, another wagon. The express men handle the box like freight, because that is what they think it is. For nearly an hour and a half, he rides on his head. Twenty-seven hours, and he never cries out.

**On screen:** Dusk-violet. A map strip runs across the top of the screen, moving from Richmond toward Philadelphia. The same crate is shown on a wagon, a train, a steamboat deck and a ferry, flipping upside down on the boat. A running counter in the corner reads HOURS IN THE BOX and climbs to 27.

**Sound:** Each vehicle has its own rhythm: hooves, rails, a paddle wheel, water. The heartbeat never stops underneath.

**Caption emphasis:** like freight, on his head, Twenty-seven hours

### Beat 5. Philadelphia (1:39 to 1:58). Spine step: Second half
**NARRATOR:** Philadelphia, about six in the morning. A knuckle raps the lid.

**A VOICE OUTSIDE:** All right?

**HENRY BROWN:** All right, sir.

**NARRATOR:** They pry it open. Henry rises, stiff and dizzy, and greets them.

**HENRY BROWN:** How do you do, gentlemen?

**NARRATOR:** Then he sings a hymn of thanks. From now on, people call him Henry Box Brown.

**On screen:** Cold-dawn light in a small office. A hand knocks on the crate. The lid lifts and Henry slowly unfolds and stands, holding the edge to keep his balance. Several men step back in astonishment. The words BOX BROWN appear above him like a name on a poster.

**Sound:** Two knocks. Nails squeaking out of wood. A short silence before his first words.

**Caption emphasis:** All right, sir, How do you do, gentlemen?

### Beat 6. The second box (1:58 to 2:28). Spine step: The surprise
**NARRATOR:** Then he does something stranger. In eighteen fifty, a new law lets slave catchers hunt him even in the North. He nearly gets caught in Providence, so he sails for England. And there, he mails himself again. He is shipped in a box from Bradford to Leeds and opened in front of a crowd. A playbill calls him King of all Mesmerisers and Professor of Magic. Night after night, he climbs out of a box while England watches.

**On screen:** A quick ship crossing an ocean on the map strip. Then a packed hall in warm-interior stage light, with a crate on the stage. The crowd is drawn as one dark mass in the seats. The lid opens and Henry steps out with both arms raised, his bandage now gone. A playbill drops into frame reading KING OF ALL MESMERISERS AND PROFESSOR OF MAGIC.

**Sound:** A ship's horn, then a murmuring crowd, then applause as he steps out.

**Caption emphasis:** he mails himself again, Professor of Magic

### Beat 7. The ending (2:28 to 2:50). Spine step: The ending
**NARRATOR:** It cost him almost everything. There is no record that he ever saw Nancy or their children again. But the box that once hid him became the stage that made people look. Once, a crate marked This side up was his only way out. After that, he made sure the whole world saw who climbed out.

**On screen:** Cold-dawn. The empty crate alone on a bare stage, lid open. Its painted words THIS SIDE UP are now the right way up for us. The camera holds on the open box until the screen fades.

**Sound:** The applause fades to silence. One last heartbeat, slow and calm.

**Caption emphasis:** almost everything, who climbed out

## Why it passes the five tests
- **TRUE:** Every fact the story rests on is in the sources table. The words spoken by Henry, the child and the voice outside are the words recorded in his narratives.
- **CLEAR:** "A man mails himself to freedom in a box, then does it again" makes sense in two seconds.
- **NEW:** The search found an animated video on the escape (hosted by World History Encyclopedia) and several children's books. The versions I found centre on the escape. This one makes the second box and his life on stage the payoff.
- **SURPRISING:** Six turns: the owner takes the money and sells the family anyway; he burns his own hand to get away; nearly an hour and a half upside down; "How do you do, gentlemen?"; he ships himself again on purpose; he becomes a professor of magic.
- **IMMERSIVE:** It opens inside the box and returns there, and the viewer lives each stage of the journey with him.

## Notes on accuracy
- **Two different box sizes are recorded.** His 1851 narrative gives three feet one inch by two feet six inches by two feet. Wikipedia gives three feet by two feet eight inches by two feet. The script says "about three feet across", which fits both.
- **Air holes:** His narrative mentions three small holes. Wikipedia mentions a single air hole. The script follows his own account.
- **"Down to the bone"** comes from Wikipedia. His own narrative says only that he used too much acid and made it worse.
- **The narratives may not be in his own words.** The 1849 narrative was probably ghostwritten by Charles Stearns, according to the historian Andrew Delbanco, as cited by World History Encyclopedia. The quotations are as recorded, not necessarily word for word.
- **The Leeds shipment has no date** in the source, so the script says only "there, he mails himself again".
- **The playbill wording** comes from one 1868 playbill. The script says "a playbill", not "playbills".
- **A hard fact left out of the narration.** Both Wikipedia and World History Encyclopedia say that after his escape, Nancy's new owner offered to sell her and the children to Brown, and he refused. The sources give no reason. It was left out because it needs more context than a three-minute video can give. Decide whether you want it in a longer version.
- **Showing slavery:** The chained line is shown with the people upright and dignified. No violence or injury is shown on screen. The acid scene shows only a bottle and a bandage.
- **Skin tones:** Henry, Nancy, the children and James Smith should be drawn with the darker skin tones from the house style, so they are clearly Black.
