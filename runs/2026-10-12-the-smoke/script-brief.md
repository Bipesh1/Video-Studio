# THE HOUSE: Orientation Tapes · Tape 01

## A. HEADER

- **Tape:** 01
- **Title:** How to Make Someone Sit in Smoke
- **Product:** People copy calm faces
- **Real game or effect:** The bystander effect (Latané and Darley's smoke-filled room study)
- **Subject:** Copycat · Subject 112 · wants: sit ten minutes, take the twenty, go home
- **Second person:** The Cheat. He works for The House: paid one gold coin to stay calm.
- **Back row:** House Actors (gold badges), standing calm the whole tape
- **The Bomb:** Smoke is coming, and every calm face in the room is paid to stay calm.
- **The one thing that moves one way:** The smoke. It creeps under the door, rises to the table, then fills the room to his shoulders. The corner counter counts seconds in smoke.
- **On the table:** a twenty dollar note, one gold coin. In the room: the door, the wall clock, smoke.
- **Needs drawing:** nothing
- **Length:** 120 seconds · 259 spoken words · Trainer's share: 127 words (49%)

## B. SCENE CHAIN

1. The Trainer asks the viewer how long they would sit in smoke, and names today's trick.
2. Because the trick needs a group, the room is set: a Copycat who wants an easy twenty, a Cheat paid in gold, and a back row of House Actors.
3. Because everyone is in place, smoke starts to creep under the door.
4. Because the Copycat is unsure, he checks the faces around him, and every face is calm.
5. Because nobody moves, he decides it is nothing, even as the smoke rises to the table.
6. Because he has now said "fine" out loud, admitting fear would mean looking stupid.
7. Because the smoke reaches his shoulders and still nobody moves, he chooses to stay.
8. Because he stayed, the Trainer shows the real 1968 numbers behind the trick.
9. Because the trick is now exposed, the Leak tells the viewer how to break it.

**SCENE 5: The choice** · Starts: smoke at his shoulders · Ends: he stays

## D. FACT SHEET

- **75% alone, 10% with two calm strangers.** Source: Bibb Latané and John M. Darley, "Group inhibition of bystander intervention in emergencies," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 10(3), 215 to 221, 1968. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0026570. What it found: students filling in a questionnaire as smoke was puffed through a wall vent. Alone, 75% left to report it within the six-minute window. Sitting with two actors told to notice the smoke and then ignore it, only 10% reported it (1 person out of 10). In groups of three real participants, 38% of groups reported it.
- **What the scene invents:** the waiting room, the twenty dollars, the House, the Cheat and the back row of five calm people are invented for the tape. The real study had a questionnaire, a vent and two calm actors. The tape never says the study happened the way the scene shows it.
- **Fair-play note (not spoken):** the smoke study used actors paid to stay calm, which is exactly the trick the House uses. Real-world footage studies of public conflicts find someone usually does step in, so the effect is strongest when calm faces are faked or everyone is unsure. The Leak's line "Calm faces are guessing too" is built on that: once one person speaks, the room is no longer reading calm faces.
