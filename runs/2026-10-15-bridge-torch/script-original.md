TITLE: How to Get Four People Across a Bridge Before the Torch Dies
THE TWIST IN ONE SENTENCE: The obvious plan of letting the fastest person walk everyone over takes 19 minutes and fails, but sending the two slowest across together gets everyone over in exactly 17.
WHO WE FOLLOW: the Saint. The Cheat is the fastest walker. Two plain grey figures: a woman carrying a heavy bag, and an old man.
THE PLACE: an old rope bridge over a deep gorge, at night, in a coming storm.
IN THE SCENE: one burning torch, a heavy bag, the far cliff across the gorge.
NEEDS DRAWING: a rope bridge over a deep gorge at night, with the far cliff on the other side; a burning torch whose flame gets smaller as the film goes on.

1. THE QUESTION
(Night. Wind howls. A rope bridge swings over a black gorge. Four people stand at the edge, lit by one torch.)
TRAINER: If the slowest person holds everyone back, who should walk with them? [BIG WORDS]
TRAINER: The smart plan fails. Watch.

2. THE RULES
(Thunder. The torch flame bends in the wind.)
TRAINER: Four people must cross this rope bridge before the storm breaks it.
TRAINER: Two fit at a time, and someone must carry the torch back each time.
(The man in the red tie holds up the torch.)
TRAINER: The man in the red tie crosses in one minute, and the woman in the scarf in two.
TRAINER: The woman with the bag needs five, the old man ten.
TRAINER: Two together walk at the slower pace.
(A clock ticks.)
TRAINER: And the torch will last *seventeen* minutes. [NUMBER: 17 minutes]

3. THE SQUEEZE
(The Saint turns to the man in the red tie.)
SAINT: You're fastest. Walk us over one at a time.
(The Cheat counts on his fingers. His face drops.) [REACTION: shock]
TRAINER: Ten, five and two minutes, plus two runs back, makes *nineteen* minutes.
CHEAT: The torch only lasts seventeen minutes.
(Thunder, closer now. The ropes creak. The flame is shorter.)
CHEAT: So we leave the old man. Without him, we make it easily. [CLIFFHANGER]
(The old man looks down at his feet. The woman with the bag steps away from the Cheat.)
SAINT: Nobody stays behind.
SAINT (thinks): Every trip with the old man takes ten minutes, whoever walks beside him.

4. THE TWIST
(Rain starts. The torch spits.)
TRAINER: So who should walk beside the old man? [CLIFFHANGER]
(Silence.)
(The Saint looks from the old man to the woman with the bag.)
SAINT (thinks): Put the two slow ones together, and we only wait once.
(The two fastest cross. The man in the red tie runs back with the torch.)
TRAINER: The two fastest cross in two minutes. The man in the red tie runs back in one.
(The old man and the woman with the bag cross together, the torch held high. The ropes creak.)
TRAINER: Then the two slowest cross *together*, in ten.
TRAINER: The woman in the scarf returns in two, and the two fastest cross again in two.
(The last two step onto the far cliff. The torch flickers and goes out.) [WHAT YOU GET: Fastest walks everyone / 19 minutes, the torch dies // Slowest walk together / 17 minutes, everyone across]
TRAINER: That is exactly *seventeen* minutes.
TRAINER: The old man's ten minutes were coming anyway, so the five fit inside them.

5. IT WAS REAL
(The dark room. One bare bulb. The Trainer at his table.)
TRAINER: This is a real puzzle. Saul Levmore and Elizabeth Cook printed it in a book in 1981.
TRAINER: In 2002, the mathematician Günter Rote proved the fastest way for any group.
TRAINER: You make the first plan every time you spread slow jobs across your day. [EVERYONE]

6. THE ANSWER
OUTSIDE VOICE: Start your slowest jobs at the same time, so you only wait once. [CARD: Slow jobs? / Start them together. / Wait once.]
TRAINER: So who should walk with the slowest person? The next slowest *one*.

SOURCES:
- Saul X. Levmore and Elizabeth Early Cook, Super Strategies for Puzzles and Games, Doubleday, 1981. The earliest known printed version of the bridge puzzle: four people, one light, a bridge that holds two at a time.
- Günter Rote, "Crossing the Bridge at Night", Bulletin of the EATCS, No. 78, October 2002, pages 241 to 246. Solves the puzzle for any number of people with any walking times. https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/rote/Papers/abstract/Crossing+the+bridge+at+night.html
