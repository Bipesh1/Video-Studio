# What's the Catch?: how to find topics

## Topic finder (v1.0)

For Biku: paste everything under the line into an AI (one with web search if you can) and list your earlier videos at the bottom. It gives you ten ideas to choose from, each scored for how much people will watch, comment and share. Take the one you like to the script guide, `prompts/fine-print.md`.

Written on 2026-10-16 with what we learned on the game theory channel: people stay for something they recognise in one second and a surprise they want to tell someone, and they comment when the video touches their own money.

---

You are finding topics for What's the Catch?, a channel of two-minute vertical explainer videos about the hidden money in everyday things: why cinema popcorn costs ten dollars, why airlines sell more seats than the plane has, why the printer was cheap and the ink is not. Each video follows the money step by step in flat, bold animated graphics, told by one narrator in plain words a stranger can follow, and ends with one thing the viewer can do.

## What a good topic is

**Something almost everyone has paid for, where the real money is somewhere they never looked.** The price everyone sees is not where the business makes its money, or the thing that looks free is paid for some other way. The catch can be said in one surprising sentence.

Good examples:

- Cinema popcorn costs ten dollars because the ticket money mostly goes to the film studio, so the snacks pay for the cinema.
- Printers are sold cheap because the ink is where the money is.
- Airlines sell more seats than the plane has because some people always miss the flight.
- Free phone games make most of their money from a small share of players who spend a lot.
- Supermarkets put the milk at the back so you walk past everything else.

Not a good topic: a story about one company's scandal, an investing tip, anything that changes by the week (a share price, a sale), or anything you cannot back with a real, published number.

## What makes people watch, comment and share

Score every idea from 1 to 5 on each of these four tests, and offer only ideas that score at least 3 on all four.

1. **The one-second test.** A viewer recognises the thing at once, from a picture alone: a popcorn bucket, a printer, a plane seat, a gym card, a hotel minibar. Nothing needs explaining before they care.
2. **The "wait, what?" test.** The catch surprises most people, and they feel slightly fooled when they hear it. If most viewers already know it, it is weak.
3. **The comment test.** Ask: what would a viewer type underneath? The best topics make them share their own story ("I paid $14 for a hot dog at the stadium"), add a place they have seen the same trick, or argue about whether it is fair. If you cannot write three real comments people would leave, the idea is weak.
4. **The share test.** Would someone send it to a friend or tag them ("this is why you never use the minibar")? Topics about food, travel, phones, shopping and going out share best.

And two more things every idea needs, so it can be shown and not just told:

5. **The money can be followed.** You can draw where the money goes in three to five steps: who pays, who gets each part, and where the real profit is. A path of money is what the video shows.
6. **There is one useful thing to do.** A legal, safe, simple move the viewer can make once they know the catch (eat before the film, buy third-party ink, check the seat rules, set a reminder to cancel).

## What every idea must have

1. **Real, checkable numbers.** A price, a share, a cost or a count, each from a named source: a company's own annual report, a government body or regulator, a study, or a well-known news report. Say who published it and the year. If you are not sure of a number, leave it out. Never invent one.
2. **Plain words.** The catch can be said without business words ("margin", "loss leader", "price discrimination") unless the same sentence says what they mean.
3. **Fair to everyone.** Explain how the business works; do not accuse a named company of anything a source does not say.
4. **One place to draw.** The video plays in one everyday place (a cinema, an airport, a supermarket, a hotel room) where the money can be shown moving.
5. **It holds for two minutes.** There is enough to explain in about six short steps: the question, the moment, follow the money, the trick, the proof, your move.
6. **Not already made.** Check the list of earlier videos at the bottom; no repeats and no near-repeats.

## What to give back

Ten ideas, best first. For each:

- **Title:** a question about something everyone has paid for, with the price in it if possible, 12 words or fewer ("Why does cinema popcorn cost ten dollars?")
- **The catch in one sentence**
- **The money path:** three to five steps, who pays and who gets what
- **The numbers** you would use, each with its source (who, what, year) and how sure you are (sure / fairly sure)
- **The place** it plays in, and the one or two things to draw that everyone would recognise
- **Your move:** the one thing the viewer can do
- **Three comments** real viewers would leave under it
- **The four scores:** one-second, "wait, what?", comment, share (1 to 5 each)

Then say which one you would make first and why, in two lines.
