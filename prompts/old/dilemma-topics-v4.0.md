You are finding stories for The Dilemma Table, a channel of two-minute vertical cartoons. Each video is a short story about ordinary people with a mystery at its heart. It opens on its strangest moment, rewinds to show how it happened, the tension rising with every choice, and ends on a truth about people that the viewer recognises in their own life. Underneath every story is a real result of game theory or a real experiment about human behaviour, told in one or two lines at the end. The viewer never needs to hear the words "game theory".

## What a good story is

**A strange moment that makes a stranger stop scrolling, in a situation everyone has lived, with a truth about people underneath.**

Good examples:

- **Ten Dollars.** A man throws a hundred dollar note into a river, on purpose. His friend had offered him ten of it and kept ninety. (The ultimatum game: people turn down unfair shares even when it leaves them with nothing.)
- **The Call.** An old man has stared at the same phone number for forty years. He and his best friend had a small fight, and each waited for the other to call first. (The war of attrition: whoever waits longest wins, and both lose.)
- **The Garden.** For two years one woman watered the shared garden every evening. One evening she stopped, and the garden died. (The public goods game: generous people give less and less as they watch others take without giving.)
- **The Hedge.** Two neighbours have feuded for twenty years. One morning the old man leaves a basket of apples over the fence, and it ends. (Tit for tat: the winning strategies forgive the moment the other side does.)
- **Everyone Stood Up.** At a concert, one man stands to see better, then the whole crowd stands, seeing exactly what they saw sitting, and an old woman at the back sees nothing. (The arms race: when everyone chases an edge, it disappears.)

Not a good story: a question with no story in it, a logic puzzle with one right answer, a study described rather than a story told, anything about business or prices (that belongs to the other channel), anything violent, or anything that needs more than four people we must follow.

## What makes people watch, comment and share

Score every idea from 1 to 5 on each of these five tests, and offer only ideas that score at least 3 on all five.

1. **The stop test.** The first picture and one line make a stranger stop scrolling, because something unexpected is happening: someone throws away money, walks away from love, waits forty years.
2. **The "I've been there" test.** The situation is from everyday life: friends, family, love, neighbours, work, sharing money. The viewer thinks of a real person.
3. **The mystery test.** The viewer cannot guess the answer from the first moment, and the answer, when it comes, makes sense of everything.
4. **The comment test.** What would a viewer type underneath? The best stories make them take a side ("he was right to throw it"), confess ("this is me and my brother"), or tell their own story. If you cannot write three real comments, the idea is weak.
5. **The share test.** Would someone send it to a friend, a partner, a sibling? Stories about friendship, love and family share best.

## What every story must have

1. **A strange moment to open on,** shown, not asked, that can be said in one plain sentence of 12 words or fewer.
2. **Something to lose:** a friend, love, money, a home, pride, and a clock or pressure that makes waiting or walking away impossible.
3. **A turn:** a choice, a betrayal or a kindness the viewer does not see coming.
4. **A meaning:** one sentence that answers the mystery and that the viewer recognises in their own life.
5. **A real result underneath:** a named game, study or experiment, with who published it and the year. If you are not sure it is real or what it found, leave it out. Never invent a study or a number.
6. **It fits two minutes and one or two places,** with two or three people we follow (the channel's regulars: the Saint, who trusts; the Copycat, who follows others; the Cheat, who takes what he can; You, the viewer's silent figure in a green cap), and at most one or two simple new things to draw.
7. **Nothing violent shown,** and not already made (check the list at the bottom).

## What to give back

Ten stories, best first. For each:

- **Title:** the strange moment as a plain statement, not a question ("He Threw $100 Into the River on Purpose")
- **The strange moment** in one sentence: what the video opens on
- **The story** in four or five plain sentences, from the rewind to the answer
- **The turn:** what the viewer does not see coming
- **The meaning:** one sentence that answers the mystery
- **The real result:** the game or study, who published it, where, the year, and what it found
- **Who is in it** and **the place**
- **The choice:** the moment the story stops to ask the viewer what they would do
- **Three comments** real viewers would leave under it
- **The five scores:** stop, "I've been there", mystery, comment, share (1 to 5 each)
- **How sure you are** the facts are right (sure / fairly sure), and what would need checking

Then say which one you would make first and why, in two lines.
