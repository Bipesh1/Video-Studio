# Black history page: how to write a script

## Script guide (v1.0, 2026-10-24)

For Biku: paste everything under the line into whichever AI writes the script (one that can search the web, if you can), and fill in the INPUTS at the bottom. This page is its own: nothing from What's the Catch? or the game theory videos applies here.

---

## 0. Before you write: research, then the package

**Research first, and keep a fact sheet.** Use official and scholarly sources before anything else: the National Park Service, the Library of Congress, the National Archives, the Smithsonian's National Museum of African American History and Culture, the House of Representatives' history pages, university and museum pages, the person's own words (letters, testimony, autobiography) and newspapers of the time. Popular articles and social posts are only leads to check.

Write every fact you will use as one line, marked:

- **sure**: two good sources agree;
- **disputed**: sources differ (say how);
- **legend**: told often but not documented. Legends never go in the script as fact.

Then the package, before a word of script:

```
TITLE: (plain words, 70 characters at most)
THE MOMENT: (the story's boldest true moment, the picture of the first 3 seconds)
THE FIRST LINE: (that moment in at most 12 plain words)
THE PROMISE: (what the viewer will know by the end)
WHY A STRANGER WATCHES: (one sentence; "it is important" is not a reason)
COVER: (one picture and up to five big words)
```

## 1. The one rule

**True, clear and dignified.** A stranger scrolling past must follow every sentence the first time they hear it, and see it on screen as it is said; every fact must be one the sources support; the person is shown as the hero of their own story, never as a victim on display.

## 2. What the video is

A vertical Reel of **about 75 to 90 seconds** (a little longer is fine when the story needs it), **about 180 to 220 spoken words**, told by one narrator, deep and unhurried, like someone who respects the story. One person or one moment, told as it happened and from inside it. No one else speaks, unless it is a documented quote, read as a quote, with its source.

Good stories for this page (see `prompts/black-history-topics.md`): escapes and heists, firsts, inventors and scientists, rulers and empires, soldiers and sailors, builders of communities, artists and athletes, and the everyday courage that history books skip.

## 3. How to write every line

1. Full, plain sentences. One step to a line, each line one picture.
2. Name the person, the place and the year early, and say how long ago it was if it helps ("over 160 years ago").
3. Name every thing the picture shows, so it appears on its word.
4. Numbers said the way people say them, with what they count.
5. Specialist words explained in the same sentence ("the Union blockade, the Northern warships waiting outside the harbor").
6. Use the words of the time only where needed and explained; never use slurs, even quoted.
7. No em dashes, no emoji. Mark one important word a line with asterisks for the captions.

## 4. The shape: chosen for the story

Only two things are fixed: **the boldest true moment comes first, and what happened next comes last.** Between them, use what this story needs, never the same build twice in a row:

| Shape                 | Good for                                   | How it goes                                                                    |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| The night it happened | Escapes, rescues, heists, one decisive day | From inside the moment, with a clock or a watcher; the plan told as it unfolds |
| The rise              | Firsts, builders, inventors                | Where they started, what stood in the way, the moment it turned                |
| The hidden truth      | Something everyone thinks they know        | The usual story, the part left out, what really happened                       |
| Then and now          | A place or a community                     | What it was, what happened to it, what is there today                          |

**Mark the beats.** After a line that lands a turn or a shock, write `[BEAT]` on its own line; the picture holds about two seconds there. Four at most.

**The ending:** what happened next, why it matters, and either one line that stays with the viewer or a question people want to answer in the comments ("Why isn't his story in every school book?").

## 5. The pictures: one after each line

After each line, in square brackets, the picture. The camera travels through one drawn world of that place and time (a harbour, a town, a field, a hall), so describe where it goes, not separate slides:

- `[FLY: back out of the wheelhouse to the whole ship in the dark harbor]`
- `[LENS: on the sentry's lantern | inside: his face, watching]` (a circle opens on a thing into a close-up)
- `[X-RAY: the crate | inside: him folded in it]` (the inside shown in the same frame)
- `[MAP: Charleston harbor, 1862, the forts marked, the ship's path drawn on]`
- `[DOCUMENT: the 1862 Act of Congress, the line that names him]`
- `[DATELINE: CHARLESTON HARBOR, MAY 13, 1862]`, `[NAME: ROBERT SMALLS]`

Hardship is shown by its traces, never in detail (see the page's style guide).

## 6. Read it as a stranger before you hand it over

1. Is every fact in the script marked sure in the fact sheet, and every disputed one said as disputed?
2. Would the first line and picture alone stop me scrolling?
3. Do I know who, where and when by ten seconds?
4. Is the person the one acting in the story, not only suffering?
5. Is anything played for shock or laughs that should not be?
6. Is it about 180 to 220 words, and does the ending say what happened next?
7. Does every line name what its picture shows?
