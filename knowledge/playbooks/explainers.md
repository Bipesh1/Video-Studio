# How explainer videos win views and keep viewers (research, 2026-10-21)

For What's the Catch? (`fine-print`). Biku, 2026-10-20: "I want that channel to be an explainer type channel that explains interesting topics with good drawings and animation", and "research how explainer videos work and how those attract engagement and views and make sure this studio works like that". Most sources are creators' interviews and blog summaries, not hard data: the rules below are what the strongest channels do, not proven laws. Keep this page under 90 lines.

## 1. The idea and its package come first

- The best creators decide the idea, the title and the thumbnail before they make anything, and drop an idea they cannot package; they spend far more of their time on ideas and packaging than small creators do (Paddy Galloway, YouTube strategist for MrBeast and others: [Colin and Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway), [Creator Science](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/paddy-galloway/), [summary](https://rpn.beehiiv.com/p/content-packaging-101)).
- Title and thumbnail work as a pair ("two barrels"): the thumbnail shows, the title says, and neither repeats the other; the thumbnail must read at a glance (Derek Muller of Veritasium, [summary](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/lukeslewis)). A technical title can sink a great video: his backspin video drew most of its views off YouTube because its YouTube title was technical ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective)).
- Curiosity yes, tricks no: a title may make people curious ("legitbait") but the video must deliver exactly what it promises; a misleading package loses the viewers it wins ([Veritasium](https://www.veritasium.com/videos/2021/8/17/we-need-to-talk-about-clickbait), [thumbnail guides](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-design-tips/)).
- Thumbnails: one big clear subject, strong contrast, a few big words that read on a phone, honest to the video ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-design-tips/), [ThumbnailTest](https://thumbnailtest.com/guides/youtube-thumbnail-explained/)). A close face helps; in animation a character's face does that job.

**In the studio:** every video has `packaging.json` (title, question, promise, answer, the thumbnail's subject and words, the first ten seconds, why a stranger clicks) before its script is written; `npm run script -- check` refuses a script without it. Thumbnails follow it.

## 2. Topics that pull

- The biggest explainer videos ask what everyone has wondered: Kurzgesagt's most watched are a pandemic explained, the largest black hole, "Your Tattoo is INSIDE Your Immune System" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurzgesagt), [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/kurzgesagt)); The Infographics Show's biggest is reportedly what happens when you die ([videoexplainers](https://videoexplainers.com/?p=2335)); MinutePhysics' is the unstoppable force and the immovable object ([The Manitoban](https://themanitoban.com/?p=21616)). Space, the body, death, "what happens if", how ordinary things really work.
- Evergreen topics keep earning for years ([Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/best-faceless-youtube-niches)); trend topics spike and fade.

## 3. The first thirty seconds

- YouTube's own retention report measures how many are still watching after 30 seconds ("intro"), and shows flat stretches (they stay), slow slides (interest fading), dips (they leave or skip) and spikes (they rewatch or share) ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)).
- Biku's rule for every video holds: within the first 3 seconds the viewer knows what the video is about (CLAUDE.md). Then prove the title's promise by 10 seconds, say why it matters in the next 10; no logo, greeting or preamble ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/), [OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-hook-framework-7-openings-that-keep-viewers-watching)). A sharp early drop means the hook failed; a slow slide in the middle means the pace did.

## 4. The middle: loops that close

- Explainers that hold people work in loops: open a question, build it, answer it fully, then open the next, smaller one (Ben Kitchen and George Blackman's method, [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-scriptwriting-methods-compared/)). One or two bigger open loops ("we'll come back to the strangest part") at most; more and viewers stop trusting them ([Marketing Monk](https://www.marketingmonk.so/tools/youtube-script-generator)).
- A re-hook in the middle, sections that end a little before you expect, the payoff at the end ([OutlierKit template](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-script-template/)).
- Smooth visual transitions keep momentum; fades to black lose it ([George Blackman](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/retention-review-7-he-fixed-retention?open=false)). The journey camera already does this.

## 5. Humour, a character, a voice

- OverSimplified, MinutePhysics and AsapSCIENCE all pair real facts with jokes, simple recurring characters and a snappy narrator; the jokes "earn attention" and let big ideas land ([Podcastle](https://podcastle.ai/blog/who-is-oversimplified), [MinuteEarth](https://minuteearth.com/about), [The Manitoban](https://themanitoban.com/?p=21616)).
- Studies are mixed, but humour that is about the subject helps recall and motivation, while jokes unrelated to it do little ([UNIMAS EEG study](https://ir.unimas.my/id/eprint/49596/), [Purdue](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/open_access_dissertations/217), [AACSB](https://www.aacsb.edu/insights/2021/july/using-comedy-to-teach-serious-business)). So: jokes that grow out of the topic, never gags dropped in.
- Kurzgesagt's own account: deep research with experts and a source sheet, a script of about a dozen drafts that teaches "often without them noticing", visual metaphors sketched before any drawing, "quirky and relatable" characters, one narrator's steady tone, and music made for each video ([Kurzgesagt](https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/)). Their videos take months; ours cannot, but the order of the work is the lesson.

## 6. Length and Shorts

- Biku posts mostly Shorts (2026-10-21), so the usual video is a vertical Short of about 1 minute, a little longer is fine (2026-10-22; it was around 2 minutes until the fat Short lost half its viewers by a minute): the hook in the first line, two quick loops, a last line that leads back to the first so it plays round again. A long video (4 to 6 minutes, as MinutePhysics grew to: [TV Tropes](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/WebVideo/MinutePhysics)) only when he asks.
- Shorts audiences often do not carry over to long videos; Shorts help when they preview a long video's topic with an open question and link to it. One large study found education channels mixing Shorts at about a quarter to two fifths of uploads gained more subscribers ([AIR, 18,000 channels](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels), [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/convert-shorts-viewers-to-subscribers)). So: each long video gives one Short cut from its strongest moment, ending on its open question.

## 7. Quality over count

- Kurzgesagt makes about 12 to 15 videos a year; OverSimplified a few ([Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/top-faceless-youtube-channels/), [Podcastle](https://podcastle.ai/blog/who-is-oversimplified)). People wait for a channel whose every video is excellent. A finished video that does not meet the bar is not posted.

## 8. Read the numbers after

- After each video: the 30-second intro figure, the shape of the retention line, the click-through rate of the thumbnail ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)). Ask Biku for them from YouTube Studio; write what they show into the fine-print playbook's lessons.

## 9. Sound: its own for every video, a Short's not a long video's

Biku, 2026-10-21: every What's the Catch? Short "has the same background sounds"; "it should be different according to shorts or videos". It was so: the music followed the format's six beats (curious, upbeat, tense, reflective, hope in the same order) and the mix always took the same seed, so every mood played the same tune in every video, and every picture in a journey landed with the same pop.

What the strongest explainers do (mostly creator guides and sound-tool blogs, so habits rather than rules):

- Kurzgesagt writes new music for every video, composed to the finished picture, for its own mood and to lift its key moments; the sound effects are a layer viewers barely notice and would miss ([Kurzgesagt](https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/), [a transcript of their making-of](https://www.lingq.com/en/learn-english-online/courses/689474/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-120-4887128/)).
- In a Short the first second is judged by ear as well as eye: open on a hit, not a slow fade; keep the pace tight; the picture and captions must carry it with the sound off ([AutoShorts](https://www.autoshorts.app/en/blog/sound-design-shorts-audio-tricks), [ShortGenius](https://shortgenius.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices), [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/youtube-marketing/how-to-get-more-views-on-youtube-shorts)).
- A long video plans its music section by section and changes it to mark each one, quieter under explaining ([AIR](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/advanced-retention-editing-cutting-patterns-that-keep-viewers-past-minute-8), [RouteNote](https://licensing.routenote.com/blog/?p=9589)).
- A sound lands on the frame where its thing moves, a pop for an arrival, a whoosh between scenes, a snap for emphasis (rarely), three to five takes of each in turn so the ear does not tire, never louder than it needs to be ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/use-cases/sound-effects-for-explainer-videos), [CreatorSFX](https://creatorsfx.in/blog/best-sound-effects-for-reels-and-shorts)).
- Music about 20 dB under the voice keeps speech clear ([WCAG 1.4.7](https://wcag.dock.codes/documentation/wcag-success-criteria/wcag147)).

What the studio does now:

- **Every video its own music**: the storyboard's seed writes every mood afresh, in a key and at a speed of its own (`scoreFor` in `shared/src/assembly.ts`, `coloured` in `audio/src/studio_audio/music.py`). The plan picks the instruments for the topic with `score.palette` on the storyboard: `body` (soft bells, a warm pad, a round bass), `space`, `water`, `food`, `mystery`, `bright`, or `own`.
- **The moods follow the story**, not the format's beats: choose each scene's `mood` for what happens in it, and do not copy the last video's list.
- **A drop before the reveal**: `drop: true` on the shot where the surprise lands cuts the music 0.7 s before it and brings it back with a hit. Once or twice a video.
- **A Short** (a run made only in the tall shape): opens on a hit, its music 5% quicker and 17 dB under the voice. **A long video**: no opening hit, calmer, its music 19 dB under the voice, a mood for each chapter. A storyboard can say `score.profile` itself.
- **Pictures landing** in a journey take a pop, a blip, a click in turn, each in a slightly different take.
- **Place sounds only where the place matters** to the story, never the same three in every video.
- **A change of sound is minutes**: change the plan, then `npm run make -- --run=<run> --part=<n> --time --sound --resound` lays the new mix under the finished pictures (they must be the same pictures: the video's record keeps a fingerprint of them), then `npm run qa` and `npm run output` as usual.
- Claude cannot hear: give Biku a back-to-back listening file of the old and the new.

## 10. Animation: every movement means something

A frame-by-frame study of a real Kurzgesagt opening (the drawing in tones without outlines, a camera that never stops, transitions through an eye or a burst of light, constant small acting) is in `knowledge/playbooks/kurzgesagt-study.md`; read it with this section.

Biku, 2026-10-22: "you should focus on making animation strong okay? not just random animations ... watch how Kurzgesagt does its animation. even though we have our own style we can make it better in that style". Kurzgesagt's own motion designers teach their house style around fake 3D depth with secondary action, exaggerated timing, overlapping action and paths, and the endless zoom between scenes ([Skillshare, part 1](https://www.skillshare.com/en/classes/Motion-Graphics-with-Kurzgesagt-%E2%80%93-Part-1/631970755), [part 3](https://www.skillshare.com/de/classes/motion-design-mit-kurzgesagt-teil-3/140140401)).

The rules for every Short (checked by eye on the frame strips, with the self-check's still-picture and empty-picture measures):

1. **Nothing simply appears.** A thing arrives with a motivated move: it falls, grows out of something, slides in, comes up from below, and overshoots and settles (`springIn` in `engine/src/explainer/BodyHall.tsx`; labels rise as they grow, `Tag` in `bodyKit.tsx`).
2. **Follow-through.** What hangs or is loose keeps moving after the move: a sign wobbles to rest on its stick, a beam swings, hair and cloth lag.
3. **People move with intent.** A change of pose has a wind-up, an overshoot and a settle (`poseAt` in `engine/src/rig/pose.ts`), never a jump; in a hold they breathe and shift their weight (`withLife` in `engine/src/rig/life.ts`); they react to what happens (the wrong experts shrug, the right ones cheer).
4. **Depth.** At least three layers: near, middle, far; when the camera moves, near things move more (the crowd's rows, the blurred heads near the camera); haze or soft focus on the far and the very near.
5. **Life in every frame.** Something always drifts, twinkles or breathes (dust in the light, bulbs, clouds), so no frame is a still picture.
6. **Every move explains.** A movement shows the idea (the beam tips as the oxygen lands) or it is cut; no motion just to fill time.
7. **Transitions are journeys.** Between places the camera travels through something (into the belly, out of the mouth), never a plain cut, unless the story jumps in time.
