# What Kurzgesagt's drawing and animation do: a study

Biku put a 54-second screen recording of the opening of Kurzgesagt's "Are You A Dream Of The Universe?" in the project (2026-10-22, kept in `references/kurzgesagt/recording-1.mp4`, which is not in git and never goes into a video). Studied frame by frame (stills in `references/kurzgesagt/study/`). The aim is their craft, never their characters or designs (ground rule 13).

## What happens in the 54 seconds

About ten pictures, changing every 2 to 5 seconds, joined by cuts, a zoom through an eye, a burst of light and the ground melting away:

1. A coast road, wide; the camera holds while a red car drives in round the bends; then the whole landscape swings round with the car, and the car hops a bump (wheels off the road, its shadow left on it).
2. Inside the car: the driver, medium shot; trees slide past outside the windscreen (the drive goes on); his eyes dart side to side and blink.
3. Closer on his face; the camera pushes into one eye, the iris fills the screen, the pupil becomes a window onto galaxies.
4. Space: a slow drift, stars twinkling with cross flares, galaxies turning; then the stars stretch into streaks, faster and faster, into a white burst.
5. The man on pure white, alone, moving; he fades into a glowing see-through figure.
6. The glowing figure in a dark void full of floating coloured shapes, looking at its hand.
7. Back in the car: his eyes droop, close, snap open; a "!" of lines over his head; his head jerks.
8. The camera pulls back out of the car to the road, and the ground under the road melts into purple drips over space.
9. The chapter title written letter by letter along a curving arrow that draws itself, then curls away.

## The drawing

- **No outlines at all.** Every shape is defined by flat colour against its neighbour.
- **Every shape is two or three tones of one hue:** the base, a darker shadow shape on one side (the palm's trunk, under a cliff's lip, under the cheek and nose), and a lighter highlight (a pale stripe along the car's top edge, the sunlit top of a cliff). Shadows are darker versions of the colour, never grey or black.
- **Glass and shine as shapes:** a windscreen is a pale see-through blue with two or three diagonal white stripes of reflection.
- **Rounded, chunky shapes,** simple, with a few small exact details that reward a second look (the ornament hanging from the mirror, the wipers, grass tufts on the road's edge).
- **Saturated colour, one scheme per scene:** turquoise sea, lime grass, sand yellow, hot pink car; space in deep purple with orange and blue galaxies. Warm against cool.
- **Depth:** the far things paler and simpler, the near things big, saturated and cropped by the frame (a palm at the left edge); light sources glow (stars, the ghost) with soft bloom.
- **Faces:** big eyes (white, a large coloured iris, a dark pupil, one white highlight), thick dark brows that carry the feeling, simple mouths; skin flat with a darker skin tone for the nose and cheek shadows.

## The animation

- **The camera almost never stops.** It holds briefly, then pushes in, pulls back, swings or drifts; inside the car the world slides past the windows so the drive never stops.
- **Shots change every 2 to 5 seconds,** wide to medium to close to extreme close, then out again.
- **Transitions travel through something:** into the eye and out of the pupil into space; the stars stretching into a burst of light; the ground melting into space. A plain cut is used between nearby moments (outside the car to inside).
- **Acting is small and constant:** eye darts, blinks, eyelids drooping, a startle with a burst of lines and a jerk of the head.
- **Physical motion has weight:** the car hops, lands and its shadow stays on the road; things ease in and out, with acceleration on the big moves (the push into the eye speeds up).
- **Ambient life everywhere:** stars twinkle, galaxies turn, floating shapes drift.
- **Text is animated as part of the picture:** the title writes itself along a path that draws itself.

## What this means for our videos (our own style, their craft)

1. **Stage with big subjects.** Few things, large in the frame, faces close enough to read; crowds behind, softer and smaller.
2. **A camera that keeps moving between shots of different sizes,** a new picture every 2 to 5 seconds.
3. **Transitions through things,** not cuts: into an eye, a pupil, a cell, a screen; a world that melts or bursts into the next.
4. **Acting on every face:** eye darts, blinks, a startle with lines, reactions after a cause.
5. **Shapes in two or three tones with highlights,** glow on light sources, paler far layers. Whether our style keeps its fine navy outline or goes outline-free like theirs is Biku's choice, to be shown side by side first.
6. **Animated text** that draws itself along a path, as part of the scene.

## A 1-minute Short, studied (2026-10-22)

Biku put a screen recording of a Kurzgesagt Short in the project to learn "how they make good Shorts in 1 min" (`references/kurzgesagt/short-1.mp4`, 61 s, not in git; sheets, the words with their times and frames in `references/kurzgesagt/short-1/`). Its topic is the menstrual cycle and PMS. The recording starts a few words in ("...when the tissue from the old endometrium is shed"), so its first seconds, the hook, are not in it.

**How it is told**

- One process, explained in order: the cycle's three phases (shed, rebuild, ovulation, the secretory phase), "Menstrual Cycle Complete" at 30 s. Then it turns to people: the hormones reach the whole body and brain, "up to 90% of people with a uterus" get PMS, why scientists think so, the dark side (up to 10% severe, depression), the hope (treatments), and the big picture (billions of people go through it; "we're only beginning to fully understand this extraordinary piece of biology").
- The narration never stops: 172 words in 61 seconds, 2.8 words a second, the longest pause under a second, at the turns.
- Science first, then "you", then the dark, then hope, then wonder. It ends on wonder and humility, not on a loop back to the start.

**How it is shown**

- A new picture every 2 to 3 seconds, but only about ten hard cuts: most changes are the camera moving, zooming through a magnifying circle into the ovary, a shape changing, or a pattern filling a person's silhouette (the "hormonal rollercoaster" confetti becomes the shape of a woman on her sofa).
- A game's screen on top of the science: the phase counter "1/3, 2/3, 3/3" so you know how far along you are; a bar for each hormone (estrogen yellow, progesterone pink) that fills and drains as it is said; "Menstrual Cycle Complete" like a level finished, with a small joke of a number under it ("Cycle No. 2/~400"). The same bars come back later in the brain scene, so the picture keeps one language.
- Every new word gets a small label the moment it is said (Ovaries, Endometrium, Serotonin, PMS); the captions are small, two or three words at a time.
- A colour for each part and its mood: purple and pink for the body, yellow for ovulation, deep red for the lining, dark navy with storm clouds for depression, a yellow sunburst for hope, orange with a crowd for "billions of people".
- One simple person, and only when the story turns to how it feels: on the sofa with tea, holding her face with swirls of pain, lying on a rain cloud; at the end she glows in a circle.

**What this means for our 1-minute Shorts**

1. One clear process or question, told in steps, with an on-screen counter of the steps (1/3, 2/3, 3/3) and a "done" moment at about the half.
2. A small game-like display that carries the story: a bar, a counter or a meter that changes as it is said (for the fat Short: the kilo of fat going down, the atoms counted out), and that comes back later.
3. A label for every new word as it is said; short, small captions.
4. A new picture every 2 to 3 seconds, mostly by moving the camera or changing a shape, few hard cuts.
5. A colour per part that matches its mood.
6. The second half turns to you and to people; a person appears when it becomes about how it feels.
7. About 160 to 175 words a minute with no long pauses (our narrator is slower, so about 150 to 165 words for a minute).
8. The last line widens to everyone and leaves wonder.

## A second Short, studied (2026-10-22)

`references/kurzgesagt/short-2.mp4` (48 s of a longer Short, starting and ending mid-sentence; about 4 s, 20 to 24 s, are covered by a pop-up on Biku's computer). Topic: could we live forever if failing organs could be replaced?

**How it is told:** an assumption the viewer would make ("As soon as an organ fails, we could replace it and live on for as long as we wanted, right?!"), then "Here's the bad news" (a limit of about 120 years, the DNA in our cells loses its ability to multiply, so the repairs run out), then "So what's the good news?" (aging research: slow, stop or reverse aging; 200 years "if we are being optimistic!"), then a step further ("To live even longer, we'd have to change..."). 162 words in 48 s, 3.35 words a second, almost no pauses. It talks to you ("right?!") and turns on plain signposts: the bad news, the good news.

**How it is shown:**

- Things with faces: a heart, a cancer cell and lungs, each pops in on its word, sad-faced; the organs sit in jars in a lab.
- Its own little cast does gags: two bird scientists argue at a blackboard ("Possible Lifespan: No Limit", swear symbols in a bubble), one tugs the board; a scientist with a test tube for "huge progress". A child on a bed in X-ray view has her lungs swapped by a claw for "replace it".
- Counters and bars again: "Amount of Repairs Left: 2936" counting down to 42 as the DNA unravels; three age bars from a young face to an old one with a marker for "slow", "stop" and "reverse"; a winding road of a life from 0 to 100 to 200 years with the faces ageing and a gravestone at the end.
- A dark silhouette of a person full of stars as a window: circles open in it onto the chromosome, the nerve cells, the DNA, and the camera zooms through a circle into the next scene.
- A small handwritten aside next to the narrator's joke ("If we are being optimistic!") with a little arrow.
- A new picture every 2 to 4 seconds, 12 cuts in 48 s, a colour per scene.

## What both Shorts do, and what it means for ours

1. **Dense and never still:** 2.8 to 3.4 words a second with almost no pauses; a new picture every 2 to 4 seconds. Our narrator is calmer (about 2.4 words a second), so a minute holds about 145 to 165 words; keep the pauses short and let the pictures carry the pace.
2. **A game's screen over the story:** step counters (1/3, 2/3, 3/3), meters that fill and drain, numbers that count down ("Repairs Left"), a road of a lifetime. One per Short, coming back as the story moves.
3. **Small labels on every new word**, small short captions; big words only for the opening.
4. **Signposts a stranger follows:** "Here's the bad news", "So what's the good news?", "right?!".
5. **Faces on everything** (organs, cells) and **small gags by a cast** (our mascot can play the bird scientists' part: argue, scribble, fail, try again).
6. **The circle into the body:** a silhouette or a body with a magnifying circle that opens and takes the camera through to the next scene.
7. **A colour per part**, matched to its mood.
8. **The end widens or leans on:** wonder ("this extraordinary piece of biology") or the next step ("to live even longer, we'd have to...").

## A third Short, studied for its camera (2026-10-24)

`references/kurzgesagt/short-3.mp4` (46 s; household dust: soil, pollen and fibres, dust mites, what fine particles do in the lungs and the blood, and the good side, raindrops and Saharan dust feeding the Amazon). Biku sent it after our first 1-minute Short ("Your First Memory"), whose camera he found "odd": "it should be smooth animations and movement ... most of the time it should be continuous camera shots and for few areas it can be separate frames". Measured: 3 hard cuts in 46 s, each at a complete change of subject (the mites to the man, the man to the fireplace, the deaths to the raindrop); the picture is moving 23% of the time.

How it moves between pictures without cutting:

- **Pans and tilts in one world:** along the windowsill from the plants to the garden outside, a quick swing (motion-blurred, under a second) to the sink, a slow pull back; down out of the cloud of dust onto the carpet where the mites walk, then a slide across to skin.
- **Lens in:** a small circle opens on the thing being named (the towel, the fire) and grows to fill the frame in about a third of a second; no pop, no pause.
- **Lens out:** the reverse, the close-up (a soot particle) shrinks back into a circle that lands inside the next picture (the lungs of a person), which is how they jump scale without a cut.
- **The x-ray in the same frame:** the man turns into a dark silhouette with glowing lungs, then the blood vessels draw on over it; the camera does not move.
- **Moves are quick (0.3 to 1 s) and then hold** for 2 to 4 seconds with a slow drift.

What changed in the studio (the memory Short rebuilt, 2026-10-24): one continuous camera through a world joined by doors (into the bubble and back out of it), the head as an x-ray instead of a separate set, a quick lens and a lens-out (`lens`, `lens-out` with `diveAt.r`), and a camera that glides to things named close together instead of cutting (`tooSoon: 'glide'` in `wordCamera`).
