# Story format playbook

A tale with characters who want something: a folktale, a true episode told as a story, a fable. Pick it when the video follows people through events to an ending. The binding numbers are in `formats/story.json`; this playbook says how to use the format well. Read it before every story video. Lessons from Biku's feedback are added here by `/review`; keep it under 80 lines.

## The beats, in order

- **hook**, about 10% of the video. Open on the most striking moment or line of the whole story, so the viewer has to know more. Music: curious, tense. Shots: close-up, medium.
- **setup**, about 20% of the video. Show who this is about, where they are and what they want, in as few words as it takes. Music: curious, calm, upbeat. Shots: wide, medium.
- **rising-trouble**, about 30% of the video. Put something in their way and make it worse, with a new question or danger each time. Music: tense, comic, sorrow. Shots: medium, close-up, over-shoulder.
- **turning-point**, about 15% of the video. The moment everything changes: a choice, a discovery or a reversal, shown on a face. Music: tense, hope, triumph. Shots: close-up, medium.
- **payoff**, about 15% of the video. Show what came of it and pay off what the opening promised. Music: triumph, hope, sorrow, comic. Shots: wide, medium.
- **closing-line**, about 10% of the video. End on one line worth remembering, over a picture that holds. Music: reflective, calm, hope. Shots: wide, close-up.

## Numbers

- Length: 20 to 180 seconds. A shot stays 2 to 4 seconds.
- Pace: 140 to 165 words a minute with pauses counted. A new question, turn or danger every 20 to 30 seconds.
- Pauses: 450 ms after a line, 900 ms after a big moment.
- Sound effects: medium, 6 to 14 a minute.

## Craft

- Open inside the trouble, not before it. The setup can come second.
- One person the viewer follows, one thing they want. Cut anyone the story works without.
- Each line of rising trouble makes it worse or stranger than the line before.
- The turning point is shown on a face in close-up; let it have a long pause.
- The closing line is the thought the viewer leaves with. Hold the last picture under it.

## Facts

- A story told as true keeps to what really happened, and says so when a detail is imagined.
