# Explainer format playbook

How or why something works or happened, made clear step by step. Pick it when the viewer should come away understanding one thing. The binding numbers are in `formats/explainer.json`; this playbook says how to use the format well. Read it before every explainer video. Lessons from Biku's feedback are added here by `/review`; keep it under 80 lines.

## The beats, in order

- **hook**, about 10% of the video. Ask the question the video answers, in its most surprising form. Music: curious, tense. Shots: close-up, medium.
- **why-it-matters**, about 10% of the video. Say what the viewer or the world gains or loses from the answer. Music: curious, tense. Shots: medium, wide.
- **first-part**, about 25% of the video. Explain the first half of the answer with one clear picture for each idea. Music: curious, upbeat, calm. Shots: wide, medium.
- **second-part** (may be left out), about 25% of the video. Explain the rest, building on what was just shown. Music: curious, upbeat, tense. Shots: medium, close-up.
- **surprise**, about 15% of the video. The fact or turn the viewer did not see coming. Music: tense, comic, triumph. Shots: close-up, medium.
- **takeaway**, about 15% of the video. Say the answer once more in one plain sentence, and end on it. Music: reflective, hope, calm. Shots: wide, close-up.

## Numbers

- Length: 45 to 180 seconds. A shot stays 2 to 4 seconds.
- Pace: 145 to 170 words a minute with pauses counted. A new question, turn or danger every 20 to 30 seconds.
- Pauses: 400 ms after a line, 800 ms after a big moment.
- Sound effects: medium, 5 to 12 a minute.

## Craft

- The hook is the question in its most surprising form, not the topic's name.
- One idea per scene, each with one picture that could be understood with the sound off.
- Use a comparison the viewer already knows for anything large, small or abstract.
- Save the fact nobody expects for the surprise beat; do not spend it in the hook.
- The takeaway repeats the answer in one plain sentence.

## Facts

- This is a factual format: a script with no saved sources is refused.
- Every number, date and name comes from a saved source.
- A rounded figure is spoken as about, nearly or more than, never as exact.
- What is not known or is disputed is said to be so.
