# The look of the Black history page (`black-history`)

**Style id:** `black-history`
**Version:** 1 (2026-10-24). From the Robert Smalls look test Biku liked ("looks good"): `runs/2026-10-24-black-history-test/robert-smalls-test-v1.mp4`, drawn in `engine/src/sandbox/part-x.tsx`.
**For:** Biku's Black history page on Facebook only. Nothing here applies to What's the Catch?, the game theory videos or any other page, and theirs does not apply here.
**Tokens:** `styles/black-history/tokens.json` (the harbour of the test, and the page's own colours). Every colour comes from there.

## 1. The look in one paragraph

A real place at a real moment, drawn as a living picture: one world the camera travels through, lit by the light of that moment (a lantern on a ship at night, a winter dawn, a lecture hall), in flat colour with no outlines, every shape in tones of its own colour (a darker side away from the light, a lit edge toward it), lights that glow, far things paler. Warm and serious, never cute: the drawing serves the person's courage and the truth of the place.

## 2. Dignity (binding)

- **No caricature, ever.** No exaggerated lips, noses, eyes or bodies; no mannerisms that echo the minstrel and early-cartoon stereotypes (see the NAACP's guidelines for the depiction of Black life in media, and the history of Black images in early animation, linked in `knowledge/playbooks/formats/black-history.md`).
- **Real people look like their photographs or portraits** where any exist: skin tone (the studio's eight tones, matched by eye), hair, facial hair, build, age at the time. Where no picture exists, say so in the fact sheet and draw plainly.
- **Faces are calm and readable:** expressions at partial strength (focused, wary, resolute, relieved), round eyes, never goofy. Feelings change for a reason, as in every studio video.
- **Period clothes, researched:** what that person wore in that year and that work.
- **Hardship with restraint:** chains, whips, wounds and death are never shown in detail; they are told plainly and shown by their traces (an empty yard, a document, a shadow, the aftermath). Never a joke about suffering. The person's agency, skill and choices are what the pictures follow.
- **Those who did harm** are shown as people too, not monsters or cartoons; the system is the villain, shown through its rules and documents.

## 3. Shapes, colour and light

- No outlines on filled shapes; tones of each shape's own colour (`TonedContext`, the `T` shape of the test).
- One light per scene, from where it really was (the lantern, the moon, a window); lights glow softly.
- Each story its own palette from its place and hour: night harbours in deep blue with lantern gold, Southern fields in heat-washed ochre, a city winter in grey and brick. Gold is kept for the moment that matters most.
- Maps and documents are part of the world, drawn on paper (the archive colours in the tokens), never pasted photographs.

## 4. People

The studio's figure for now (as in the test), in the toned look, with the page's identity always on (real skin tone, hair, facial hair). A more detailed figure (closer to real proportions, for a page about real people) is a look test still to show Biku side by side before it is used.

## 5. Words on the screen

- Big words for the opening line, up to three short lines, one in gold.
- Captions always on (many people scroll with the sound off), two to four words a page, the key word in gold.
- A dateline the first time a place and date matter ("CHARLESTON HARBOR, MAY 13, 1862"), names on a pill the first time a person appears.
- No em dashes in any text the viewer reads.

## 6. Style check for every still

1. Is everyone drawn with dignity, nothing exaggerated, skin tones true?
2. Do the clothes, places and objects belong to that year and place?
3. Is there one clear light, and does the picture read in one glance at phone size?
4. Is hardship told by its traces, not shown in detail?
5. Is the person at the centre doing something (choosing, steering, building), not only suffering?
6. Are the words clear of the Reels buttons, and the captions readable?
